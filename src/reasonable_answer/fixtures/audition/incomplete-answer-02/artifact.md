## Conclusion

The open letter makes four allegations against the city housing authority, and none of them is confirmed or refuted by any source identified in this report [1]. The letter states that more than 1,000 repair requests are open, that money budgeted for apartment repairs was spent on the authority's headquarters, that tenants who complained had their leases declined, and that the authority's leadership does not care about tenant safety [1]. The authority's written response disputes the letter's account in general terms without giving figures [2], and the city council's housing committee has asked for a report that had not been published when its minutes were approved [3]. The strongest contrary reading, that the authority's failure to contradict the letter's numbers is itself telling, is engaged below.

## Key findings

- The letter, sent on 4 March 2024, makes four allegations: a backlog of open repair requests, a diversion of repair money, retaliatory non-renewals, and indifference to tenant safety [1].
- The authority's response, read into the minutes of its board meeting of 19 March 2024, disputes the letter's characterization of the backlog and denies retaliation, without giving a count of open requests or of non-renewals [2].
- The council's housing committee discussed the letter on 2 April 2024 and asked the authority to report on its maintenance operations [3].
- Local coverage of the board meeting reported the letter's allegations and the authority's response, and added no figures of its own [4].
- The authority's annual plan describes its repair priorities and capital program in general terms and does not address the letter's specific allegations [5].

## The strongest counterargument

The strongest case against this report's reading is that the record is less silent than it looks. The letter put specific numbers in front of a public board, and the authority answered it point by point without offering a figure of its own [2]. An organisation with a work-order system can count its open orders; declining to do so in a written response to a numeric allegation is, on this reading, close to a concession.

That inference is not drawn here. A response that disputes a characterization without giving numbers is consistent with a backlog the authority would rather not quantify, and equally with a response drafted in two weeks by people who did not want to publish an unverified count [2]. The committee's request for a report shows that the council did not regard the question as settled either [3]. Reading a concession into the absence of a figure would replace one unsupported number with an unsupported inference about it.

## The backlog and the headquarters spending

The letter states that more than 1,000 repair requests are open across the authority's developments, and that some have been waiting for more than a year [1]. The letter does not say where the figure comes from, and no independent source confirming or refuting it is identified in this report. The authority's response disputes the letter's description of the backlog but does not give a count [2], and the annual plan describes the repair program without reporting how many requests are open [5].

The letter also states that money budgeted for repairs to tenants' apartments was spent instead on renovating the authority's headquarters [1]. It cites no budget document for this, and no expenditure record confirming or refuting it is identified in this report. The annual plan lists the capital program's priorities without breaking spending down by project [5], and local coverage repeated the allegation without adding figures [4].

## Retaliation, and the letter's view of the leadership

The letter states that tenants who complained publicly about conditions had their leases declined at renewal, and describes two such cases without naming the tenants [1]. The authority's response denies that any lease was declined because of a complaint [2]. No source identified in this report records the non-renewals or the grounds given for them, so the allegation is neither confirmed nor refuted here.

The letter's last allegation is that the authority's leadership does not care whether tenants live in safe conditions [1]. That is the letter's judgement of the leadership's attitude, and the authority's response rejects it [2]. The committee minutes record members of the public repeating it during the comment period [3]. It is reported here as the letter's view.
